IBM Quantum, Qiskit and Fall Fest

Four names get used a lot around this event. What each one is, and how they relate to each other.

IBM Quantum

IBM's quantum computing division. In 2016 they put a real quantum computer on the internet for anyone to use, which had never been done before. That is the anniversary this year's Fall Fest marks: ten years of quantum on the cloud.

Qiskit

IBM Quantum's open-source Python toolkit for writing quantum programs. Free, and the thing you will actually be typing on Saturday. If you can write basic Python, a quantum circuit is about five lines.

Qiskit Fall Fest

A global series of student-run quantum events that IBM Quantum supports each fall. Around 200 universities are taking part in 2026. Last year's edition drew more than 32,000 participants across 150 organizers worldwide.

Fall Fest Connecticut

This event. Connecticut's edition, hosted by UConn Quantum Computing at Storrs. You do not need to be a UConn student to take part, and you do not need to have done any quantum computing before. Students from any Connecticut university are welcome.

UConn Quantum Computing

A student organization at UConn Storrs for people who want to use quantum computers, not just read about them. Open to any UConn student, any major, whether or not you have written code before.

The three days

Each day removes a different reason not to come. Come to one, two, or all three. Thursday and Friday are short evening sessions, and nothing is a prerequisite for anything else.

Thu 15 Oct6:00 to 8:15 PM

Meet

A careers and research panel, and time to network with industry professionals working in quantum.

Fri 16 Oct5:00 to 9:00 PM

Learn

Quantum and Qiskit from scratch, a talk from an IBM Quantum speaker, and your first circuit on a real quantum computer.

Sat 17 Oct10:00 AM to 5:00 PM

Build

The hackathon. Build something with mentors on hand all day, demo it in the afternoon, and stay for the awards.

Timings, challenges and speakers will be announced closer to the event. Register and we will email you when they are.

Questions

I know nothing about quantum computing. Is this really for me?

Yes, and specifically so. About half the places at this event are held for people who have never studied quantum computing. Friday evening teaches you what you need, the Saturday has work pitched squarely at complete beginners, and mentors are on hand all day.

Do I have to come to all three days?

No. Each day works on its own. Come Saturday only if that is all you can make, and plenty of people will. That said, Friday evening is the fastest route to arriving on Saturday ready to build, and it is where teams form.

Do I need a team?

No. Teams form on Friday evening, and we will match anyone who arrives on Saturday without one. You can also work solo.

I am not a UConn student. Can I come?

Yes. This is a statewide event and students from any Connecticut university are welcome. You will need to be able to travel to Storrs in person, and there is a question about that on the registration form.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Registration is free and food is provided on all three days.

What is Qiskit?

Qiskit is IBM's open-source Python toolkit for writing quantum programs. If you can write basic Python, you can write a quantum circuit in about five lines. If you cannot write Python yet, Friday will get you far enough.

Will there be prizes?

Yes, along with certificates of participation. Details closer to the event.

How do I join UConn Quantum Computing afterwards?

Turn up to a meeting. There is no application and no prerequisite. Ask any organizer at the event, or email us.

Registration

Free, open to students across Connecticut, and designed for people starting from zero. Saturday places are limited by room capacity, so register early.
